Purpose: Define the business problem, operational context, expected value, constraints, and measurable outcomes that justify the development of Signaly.

⸻

1. Executive Summary

Modern organisations generate large volumes of operational signals across support systems, application logs, monitoring platforms, customer interactions, workflow tools, and internal services.

The problem is rarely a lack of data.

The problem is determining which signals matter, what they mean, and what action should be taken next.

Important events may be distributed across multiple systems, buried within noisy data, duplicated across tools, or presented without sufficient context. Operations teams must manually investigate these signals, correlate information, determine severity, and decide whether an issue requires action.

This creates several operational problems:

* high alert and information volume;
* fragmented operational context;
* repetitive manual investigation;
* inconsistent prioritisation;
* delayed identification of important events;
* limited explanation for automated decisions;
* poor visibility into why particular actions were recommended.

Signaly is proposed as an AI-assisted operational intelligence platform that transforms fragmented operational signals into structured, prioritised, and explainable insights.

Rather than acting as an uncontrolled autonomous decision-maker, Signaly is designed around human-supervised AI assistance.

The platform will ingest operational events, enrich and correlate them, assess their relevance and severity, generate evidence-grounded summaries, recommend appropriate actions, and preserve the reasoning and evidence associated with each recommendation.

The objective is not simply to introduce AI into an operational workflow.

The objective is to determine whether AI can measurably reduce investigation effort and improve operational decision quality without sacrificing reliability, explainability, or human control.

2. Business Context

2.1 Fictional Client Organisation

For the purpose of developing and evaluating Signaly, the project assumes a fictional organisation:

Northstar Digital Services (NDS)

NDS is a UK-based digital services company operating several customer-facing applications and internal platforms.

Its technology environment includes:

* customer-facing web applications;
* internal APIs and microservices;
* cloud infrastructure;
* application monitoring;
* service desk systems;
* customer support channels;
* operational dashboards;
* incident-management workflows.

The organisation has experienced significant growth.

As its infrastructure and customer base have expanded, the volume of operational information generated by its systems has increased substantially.

However, the processes used to investigate and prioritise operational events remain heavily dependent on manual analysis.

4. Problem Statement

NDS lacks a unified mechanism for converting fragmented operational signals into contextualised, prioritised, and explainable decision support.

Existing tools are effective at generating alerts, logs, tickets, and metrics but often leave the interpretation of those signals to human operators.

As a result, analysts spend significant time answering questions such as:

* Is this event genuinely important?
* Has something similar happened recently?
* Are multiple alerts actually describing the same underlying issue?
* Which systems or customers are affected?
* How severe is the problem?
* What evidence supports that assessment?
* What action should be taken next?
* How confident should we be in that recommendation?

The resulting investigation overhead increases operational cost and can delay responses to important events.

7. Intended Product Capabilities

The proposed platform should eventually support the following capabilities.

Signal ingestion

Accept operational events from multiple simulated or real data sources through structured interfaces.

Signal normalisation

Convert heterogeneous incoming events into a consistent internal representation.

Event enrichment

Attach contextual information such as:

* source;
* timestamp;
* affected service;
* environment;
* historical patterns;
* related events;
* severity indicators.

Correlation

Identify signals that may belong to the same underlying operational event.

Prioritisation

Estimate the importance of an event using deterministic and/or machine-learning approaches.

AI-assisted summarisation

Generate concise operational summaries from available evidence.

Recommendation generation

Suggest appropriate next actions while clearly distinguishing recommendations from confirmed facts.

Confidence representation

Communicate uncertainty rather than presenting every AI output as equally reliable.

Evidence traceability

Allow users to inspect the evidence contributing to an AI-generated conclusion.

Human feedback

Capture whether operators accept, reject, or modify recommendations.

Auditability

Maintain records of significant system decisions and human interventions.

9. Current-State Baseline

For the portfolio implementation, Signaly will establish a controlled baseline using simulated operational scenarios.

The baseline represents an analyst working without Signaly.

The analyst receives raw events and must:

1. inspect the event;
2. identify relevant context;
3. determine related signals;
4. estimate severity;
5. determine priority;
6. recommend an action.

The Signaly-assisted workflow will then be evaluated against this baseline.

This prevents the project from relying solely on statements such as:

“The AI appears useful.”

Instead, the project will attempt to measure whether the system actually improves the workflow.

10. Business Hypothesis

The primary business hypothesis is:

Providing operators with correlated, contextualised, prioritised, and evidence-grounded AI assistance will reduce investigation effort while maintaining or improving decision quality.

This hypothesis will later be decomposed into measurable technical and operational hypotheses.

13. AI Value Boundary

Not every component of Signaly requires artificial intelligence.

This distinction is intentional.

Traditional software should be preferred when behaviour can be expressed reliably using deterministic logic.

Potential AI applications include:

* semantic similarity;
* event clustering;
* anomaly detection;
* contextual summarisation;
* evidence retrieval;
* recommendation generation;
* natural-language interaction.

Traditional software remains appropriate for:

* authentication;
* authorisation;
* event validation;
* schema enforcement;
* deterministic business rules;
* audit logging;
* workflow state management;
* API contracts.

This prevents Signaly from becoming an application where AI is introduced unnecessarily.

15. Key Constraints

The initial implementation will operate under several constraints.

Data

Real enterprise operational data may not be available.

The project will therefore use controlled synthetic or public data where appropriate.

Cost

The portfolio implementation should remain inexpensive to reproduce.

Where practical, development should favour open-source tools and free development tiers.

AI Reliability

Generative models may produce unsupported or incorrect statements.

AI-generated recommendations must therefore be grounded in available evidence.

Explainability

Important recommendations should expose supporting information rather than presenting opaque outputs.

Security

Sensitive operational information must not be unnecessarily exposed to external services.

Scalability

The initial implementation will not reproduce enterprise-scale traffic but should use architecture that demonstrates realistic scaling considerations.

16. Key Assumptions

The business case currently assumes that:

* operational events can be represented using structured schemas;
* relevant historical information is available for retrieval;
* some events can be labelled for evaluation;
* analysts benefit from consolidated context;
* human review remains available for consequential decisions;
* synthetic scenarios can approximate meaningful operational workflows;
* AI assistance can be evaluated independently from full production deployment.

These assumptions will be tested where possible rather than treated as guaranteed facts.

18. Non-Goals

The first version of Signaly will not attempt to:

* replace enterprise observability platforms;
* autonomously remediate production infrastructure;
* provide unrestricted autonomous agents;
* guarantee root-cause identification;
* eliminate human operational judgement;
* reproduce the scale of a large commercial monitoring platform;
* train a foundation model from scratch.

These boundaries keep the project technically credible and achievable.

20. Success Criteria

The Signaly MVP will be considered successful if it demonstrates that:

1. operational signals can be ingested through a consistent interface;
2. events can be validated and normalised;
3. relevant contextual information can be attached;
4. related events can be correlated;
5. events can be prioritised using a documented method;
6. AI-generated summaries are grounded in available evidence;
7. recommendations include inspectable supporting evidence;
8. uncertainty or confidence is communicated;
9. human operators can accept, modify, or reject recommendations;
10. system decisions can be audited;
11. performance can be measured against a defined baseline;
12. the application can be reproduced from documented instructions.

⸻

21. Failure Criteria

The project should not be considered successful merely because an AI model produces plausible text.

The proposed approach should be reconsidered if:

* recommendations regularly contain unsupported claims;
* important events are consistently assigned low priority;
* event correlation creates unacceptable false groupings;
* generated summaries omit critical evidence;
* confidence information is misleading;
* the AI-assisted workflow provides no measurable improvement over the baseline;
* system complexity exceeds the value produced;
* outputs cannot be reproduced or evaluated consistently.

Negative results will be documented rather than hidden.

A portfolio project demonstrating that a particular AI approach does not provide sufficient value can still represent sound engineering when the conclusion is supported by evidence.
